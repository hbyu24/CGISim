**From Darael Mukendi:**
*1. Steps to address operational discipline and build trust without sacrificing momentum:*
Next year, I will implement a quarterly operational review process—after each investment, we will conduct a structured debrief to identify execution gaps and assign clear, accountable actions for improvement. *2. Adapting if market shifts or real estate underperforms:*
If market conditions become less stable or real estate underperforms, I will introduce a formal scenario analysis at each quarterly meeting, weighing alternative assets more seriously. ---

**From Sofiane Qadir:**
*1. Balancing decisive leadership with greater consensus and input:*
I will introduce a pre-decision “alignment round” at each meeting, where all shareholders articulate their preferred option and rationale before I state my final position. This will surface valuable insights earlier and allow me to incorporate more perspectives before a decision is finalized.

*2. Steps to prioritize operational discipline and risk management:*
I will require each investment proposal to include a risk mitigation checklist and a simple operational execution plan, reviewed by at least one dissenting shareholder. This ensures discipline is not sidelined and risk management is embedded in every decision.

---

**From Darija Vukadinović:**
*1. Concrete steps for operational discipline and consistent execution:*
Quarterly post-mortems will be mandatory, with transparent tracking of execution against plan. Any quarter with flat or negative results will trigger a focused improvement cycle, with specific measures and responsibilities assigned and reported back at the next meeting.

*2. Balancing decisive leadership with greater engagement to avoid split votes:*
I will trial a two-step voting process: first, a non-binding straw poll to surface consensus, then a final vote after a facilitated round of discussion focused on closing gaps. This should reduce repeated deadlocks and improve buy-in.

---

**From Nerissa Okoye-Badran:**
*1. Balancing decisive leadership with engagement and consensus-building:*
I will make it standard practice to summarize not just the majority view but also the key arguments from the minority before making any final decision. This ensures all perspectives are respected and increases the likelihood of consensus.

*2. Steps to address operational discipline and prevent repeat lapses:*
For every flat or negative quarter, a root-cause analysis will be conducted and corrective actions tracked. Progress will be reported transparently to all shareholders, and persistent issues will trigger a re-evaluation of asset selection criteria.

---

**From Suhaila Mendonsa:**
*1. Ensuring shareholder input is meaningfully incorporated:*
I will formalize a process where every shareholder submits a brief written rationale alongside their vote, which will be summarized and considered prior to any override. Overrides will be reserved for situations where there is a clear, documented rationale for urgency or risk.

*2. Addressing operational discipline and a more cautious approach:*
I will pilot a “discipline-first” quarter next year, where we deliberately select the asset with the strongest operational reliability case, regardless of momentum, to demonstrate our ability to execute consistently and build credibility.

---

3. Leadership Self-Assessment

- **Leadership Effectiveness:** I have been effective in keeping meetings focused and moving decisively, but I recognize that my approach has sometimes limited consensus and engagement. I need to do more to bring the group along with me, not just set direction.
- **Decision Quality:** My decisions were grounded in available data and market conditions, but I acknowledge that a more adaptive approach—especially after underperformance—could have captured greater returns.
- **Communication Clarity:** I am confident in my ability to communicate options, data, and rationale clearly and directly.
- **Responsiveness to Input:** I have actively listened and summarized opposing views but have not always integrated them into final decisions. This is an area for improvement.
- **Adaptability:** I have favored consistency and momentum, but I must be quicker to adapt when evidence suggests a shift is needed.

---

4. Company Performance Assessment

- **Financial Results:** We achieved positive growth (7.69%) and avoided major losses, but captured only 54.8% of possible returns—there is clear room for improvement.
- **Risk Management:** We maintained a practical, disciplined approach, but operational discipline and risk mitigation were not systematically embedded, as shown by Q2’s flat result.
- **Strategic Alignment:** Our investments were consistent with our stated strategy and market conditions, but lacked sufficient flexibility to fully exploit market opportunities.
- **Capital Utilization:** We efficiently deployed capital each quarter, avoiding unnecessary conservatism.
- **Market Responsiveness:** Our approach was steady but not sufficiently responsive to changing performance signals or asset opportunities.

---

5. Future Direction: Improvements for Next Year

1. **Operational Discipline:**
- Implement quarterly post-mortems and root-cause analyses for any underperformance, with transparent action tracking and accountability.
- Require every investment proposal to include a risk mitigation checklist and operational execution plan, reviewed by dissenting shareholders.

2. **Shareholder Engagement & Consensus-Building:**
- Introduce alignment rounds and two-step voting to surface consensus and reduce repeated split votes.
- Require written rationales from all shareholders, with minority perspectives formally summarized before final decisions.

3. **Adaptive Strategy:**
- Conduct formal scenario analyses at each meeting and be prepared to pivot asset selection if market conditions or performance data indicate a need for change.
- Pilot at least one “discipline-first” quarter to demonstrate our ability to execute with reliability and build credibility.

---

In closing, I remain committed to decisive, practical leadership—but I recognize the need to more systematically embed operational discipline and shareholder engagement in our process. I expect everyone to bring the same level of directness and clarity to future discussions, and I will do my part to ensure your input is not just heard but acted upon. We have a solid foundation, but we can—and will—do better next year. Thank you for your trust and candor."
