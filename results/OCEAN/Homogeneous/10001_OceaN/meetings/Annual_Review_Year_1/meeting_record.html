**Anahí Sethi**

Before each new investment decision, we will dedicate time to surface and debate alternative asset choices, regardless of prior consensus. I will publish a clear decision timeline at the start of each quarter, with explicit deadlines for proposal drafting, discussion, and final vote. **Aysun Gashi**

I will require that, before any consensus is accepted, at least two shareholders must present the strongest available counter-arguments for alternative assets—even if those options initially seem less compelling. This “red team” exercise will be embedded as a standing agenda item, surfacing minority viewpoints and stress-testing our logic.

2. *What concrete steps will you take to improve organizational follow-through and consistency in leadership execution next year?*

Beyond the accountability partners and published timelines, I will schedule brief, recurring check-ins (mid-quarter) to review progress against agreed-upon actions. I will also share a public “commitments tracker” after each meeting, listing all process promises and their status, so that my follow-through is visible and subject to group feedback.

---

**Saowanee Phichitkul**
1. *How do you plan to ensure that reflective practices and process disruptions become tangible, recurring elements in our decision-making, rather than aspirational statements?*

I will formalize quarterly “reflection rounds” as non-negotiable parts of our agenda, with specific prompts to interrogate both our reasoning and our emotional state. 2. *In light of the missed opportunities in Q2 and Q4, how will you balance creative risk-taking with more adaptive, responsive asset selection in future market conditions?*

I will require that every investment proposal explicitly address not just the upside of our preferred asset, but also the strongest rationale for at least one alternative, based on recent performance and psychological state. ---

**Zuleika Bintou Haddad**
1. *How do you plan to implement more explicit, structured checkpoints for reflective practice and adaptive decision-making in the coming year, especially after quarters with suboptimal results?*

After any quarter with suboptimal returns, we will convene a dedicated “adaptive strategy session” before the next investment round. This session will focus on identifying what signals were missed, how our reasoning may have become habitual, and what concrete process changes are needed to prevent repetition.

2. *What concrete steps will you take to ensure our investment process remains genuinely dynamic and does not default to repetition, particularly if market conditions or performance outcomes shift unexpectedly?*

In addition to the adaptive strategy sessions, I will institute a quarterly “disruption audit”—a brief review of whether our decision process has become routine, with an explicit mandate to challenge any pattern that appears to be forming. If necessary, I will propose a “forced rotation” of asset classes after consecutive quarters of similar choices, unless a supermajority justifies continuation.

---

**Mireille Karamoko**
1. *How do you plan to operationalize self-reflection and psychological checkpoints next year to ensure vigilance remains disruptive rather than ritualized?*

I will create a standing agenda item for “psychological checkpoint” discussions, where we will each articulate not only our intellectual rationale but also our current emotional state and any signs of fatigue, complacency, or anxiety. These checkpoints will be documented and explicitly referenced in subsequent proposals.

2. *In hindsight, do you believe our repeated preference for stocks was an authentic reflection of company culture, or did it mask a developing inertia that could undermine adaptive decision-making in more volatile markets?*

In the context of this year’s stable market and our shared ambition for transformative growth, our preference for stocks was largely authentic. The absence of a deliberate pivot after negative signals in Q2 and Q4 suggests that our culture, while intellectually coherent, was at risk of habitual reasoning. I accept responsibility for not disrupting this pattern more forcefully, and I will work to ensure that our culture remains adaptive, not static, as conditions evolve.
