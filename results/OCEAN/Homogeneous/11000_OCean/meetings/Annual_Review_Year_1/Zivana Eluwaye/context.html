**2. Direct Responses to Shareholder Questions**

*From Idara Ekwueme & Zivana Eluwaye:*

**Q1: How will you ensure that minority or dissenting viewpoints are surfaced and integrated earlier in future decision processes to improve robustness and preempt groupthink?**
I will formalize a “devil’s advocate” protocol at the outset of each decision round. Before moving to proposals, I will explicitly invite each shareholder to articulate either a dissenting perspective or a hypothetical scenario in which the prevailing view fails. Additionally, I will rotate the responsibility of challenging consensus among shareholders, ensuring that critical scrutiny is institutionalized, not incidental. This will help surface unconventional risks and force us to rigorously interrogate our assumptions before crystallizing alignment.

**Q2: What concrete measures will you implement to enhance the company’s responsiveness to changing market signals and optimize asset allocation beyond defaulting to equities in stable environments?**
I will operationalize explicit, pre-defined asset rotation triggers based on scenario analysis and performance thresholds. For example, if equities underperform relative to another asset by a set margin in any given quarter, the default for the next allocation will shift to a comparative risk-return review, with a presumption in favor of the outperforming asset unless robustly rebutted. This mechanism will be transparently documented and reviewed at each meeting to ensure accountability and dynamic responsiveness.

*From Elodia Chisombele:*

**Q1: How do you plan to operationalize earlier identification and integration of dissenting perspectives to prevent consensus-driven blind spots in future investment decisions?**
Beyond the devil’s advocate protocol, I will require a structured “minority risk round” before any proposal vote, where each shareholder must state at least one risk or alternative not yet fully explored. This will be a standing agenda item. I will also assign a “risk monitor” role—rotating quarterly—tasked with tracking and surfacing emerging dissent or overlooked alternatives during discussion.

**Q2: What specific changes will you implement in our asset allocation process to ensure more dynamic responsiveness to quarterly market signals and maximize return capture?**
I will introduce a quarterly “signal review” as a formal process step. After reviewing asset performance data, we will explicitly compare realized returns to our prior expectations and to alternative assets. If an asset underperforms by a material threshold, we will require a majority justification to retain it as the default, otherwise the allocation will shift. This ensures that inertia does not override empirical evidence.

*From Tariro Maseko:*

**Q1: How will you ensure that dissenting and unconventional perspectives are surfaced earlier and more robustly in future decision processes?**
I will embed a standing challenge segment at the start of each meeting, where all shareholders are required to articulate a contrarian view or identify a potential flaw in the consensus logic. This will be complemented by periodic “red team” exercises, where a subset of shareholders is tasked with constructing the strongest possible counter-argument to the prevailing strategy.

**Q2: What specific changes will you implement to enhance adaptive allocation in response to underperformance or shifting market signals, rather than defaulting to prior strategies?**
I will institutionalize adaptive allocation by establishing explicit, data-driven reallocation criteria. If any asset class underperforms the best available alternative by a pre-set margin, the next quarter’s allocation discussion will begin with a presumption in favor of the outperforming asset, subject to rigorous challenge. This will be codified in our decision protocols and reviewed annually for effectiveness.

*From Eniola Thandiwe Okoro:*

**Q1: How will you ensure more proactive surfacing of dissenting perspectives to avoid groupthink and enhance decision robustness in future meetings?**

I will implement quantitative performance thresholds—if stocks underperform another asset by a defined percentage, the default for the next quarter will shift to a comparative review with a bias toward the outperformer.
