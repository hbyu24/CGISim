**1. How do you plan to more concretely define and implement readiness milestones to enable timely transitions to higher-return assets in future years?**
I will work with the team to co-create a set of explicit readiness milestones, including both quantitative indicators (e.g., consecutive quarters of positive returns, error-free execution, timely reporting) and relational signals (e.g., open feedback cycles, demonstrated comfort with dissent, visible enthusiasm for ambition). These will be agreed upon at the start of the year and revisited quarterly. I will propose integrating a standing agenda item in each meeting to explicitly assess our progress against these milestones before considering asset allocation shifts.

**2. What steps will you take to ensure the company more effectively balances risk management with seizing growth opportunities when market conditions are favorable?**
I intend to introduce a “risk window” framework—where, upon meeting readiness milestones and in the presence of favorable market signals, we pre-authorize a shift to higher-return assets for a defined period. This approach will preserve our disciplined foundation while enabling us to act decisively when opportunity and preparedness align.

***From Mireille Tsegaye:***

**1. How do you plan to incorporate clearer readiness signals and operational milestones to guide responsible transitions to higher-return assets in the coming year?**

Before each investment decision, we will review this dashboard and use it to structure our discussion around whether conditions warrant a transition.

**2. What specific steps will you take to ensure earlier consensus-building and reduce extended deliberations in future investment decision processes?**
I will pilot a “pre-round” survey before key meetings, asking shareholders to signal their comfort level with various options and perceived readiness. This will surface areas of alignment and divergence early, allowing me to tailor the agenda and facilitate more focused, efficient discussions.

***From Vesna Branković:***

**1. How do you plan to help the team identify and agree on clear readiness signals or milestones for responsibly transitioning to higher-return assets in the coming year?**
I will invite the team to a dedicated workshop at the start of the year to collaboratively define what “readiness” looks like for us—combining financial, operational, and relational criteria. **2. What specific steps will you take to balance our strong foundation of stability with a more proactive approach to capturing growth opportunities if similar market conditions persist?**
I will advocate for a “dynamic allocation” policy: once readiness milestones are met, we will allocate a defined portion of capital to higher-return assets, while maintaining a core allocation to bonds for stability. ***From Ananya Sitara Venkatesan:***

**1. How do you plan to incorporate more explicit readiness indicators or milestones to support a potential transition toward higher-return assets in the coming year, while maintaining psychological safety and disciplined evaluation?**

**2. In light of the significant gap between actual and potential returns this year, what specific steps will you take to balance our emphasis on stability with a more adaptive approach to market opportunities?**
I will propose a quarterly “market scan” and “readiness check” process, where we assess both external conditions and internal preparedness. If both are favorable, we will be empowered to pivot more quickly to higher-return assets, with clear communication and documentation of rationale.

**2. In retrospect, are there specific moments where you would have advocated more strongly for a measured step toward real estate or another growth-oriented option, and how would you approach such situations differently next year?**
Yes, in Q3 and Q4, our operational performance and team confidence were strong. Next year, I will be more proactive in presenting “test the waters” strategies—such as allocating a small portion to growth assets—to help the team build comfort through experience.
