**2. Direct Responses to Shareholder Questions**

**Mahira Zhiyenbayeva:**
1. *What specific steps will you take next year to ensure that bold, minority perspectives are not just acknowledged but meaningfully integrated into our investment strategy?*
Next year, I will formalize a process whereby minority or bold proposals are not only discussed, but actively piloted—at least in a limited, structured fashion—when operational risk is manageable. For example, I propose we designate at least one quarter as an “experimental round,” where we deliberately allocate a small portion of our budget to a higher-risk asset or hybrid strategy advocated by minority voices, with clear parameters for learning and evaluation. Additionally, I will ensure that every quarterly agenda reserves time for minority perspectives to present concrete, actionable proposals with defined success metrics.

2. *How will you address the company’s low capture of available market returns and ensure we do not fall into a pattern of risk aversion that limits our growth potential?*
I will work with the board to establish pre-defined, objective benchmarks—both operational (e.g., process reliability, error rates, team confidence) and market-driven (e.g., sustained stable conditions, repeated underperformance relative to available returns)—that trigger a formal review of our risk posture. When these benchmarks are met, we will escalate risk in a disciplined, transparent way, ensuring we do not default to caution out of habit but adapt actively to opportunity.

**Nayeli Bhasin:**
1. *How do you plan to define and implement objective benchmarks for when the company should responsibly escalate risk and consider higher-return assets in future years?*
I propose we collaboratively define a set of operational and market criteria, such as:
- Three consecutive quarters of stable returns and process reliability
- Documented team readiness (via confidence surveys or process audits)
- Market signals of persistent underutilization (e.g., repeated cases where alternative assets outperform bonds by a defined margin)
When these are met, we will commit to escalating risk in the following quarter, either by increasing allocation to higher-return assets or piloting a hybrid strategy. These benchmarks will be codified in our investment policy and reviewed quarterly.

2. *What specific steps will you take to ensure minority viewpoints are integrated earlier and more substantively into the decision-making process to enhance both innovation and buy-in?*
I will introduce a formal agenda item at the start of each investment cycle for minority or outlier proposals, requiring the team to articulate not just the risks but the potential learning value and upside of these approaches. We will also experiment with “devil’s advocate” roles, rotating responsibility for challenging consensus and championing alternative strategies, to ensure early, substantive integration of dissenting ideas.

**Vesna Branković:**
1. *How do you plan to balance our proven, cautious approach with the need to capture more growth opportunities if stable market conditions continue next year?*
We will maintain our disciplined foundation, but once our objective benchmarks are met, I will advocate for a phased shift: for example, allocating a defined percentage of our quarterly investment to higher-return assets, while retaining the majority in bonds. This approach allows us to capture upside while preserving stability and learning from real market exposure.

2. *What specific steps will you take to ensure that dissenting or minority viewpoints are more fully integrated into our investment decision-making process going forward?*
I will require that each investment proposal explicitly address how dissenting views were considered, and if not adopted, why. Where feasible, we will implement controlled pilots of minority strategies, with clear learning objectives and post-mortem analysis to ensure every perspective contributes to our evolving playbook.

**Jinara Nandini:**
1. *What specific, objective criteria will you use to determine when the company is ready to increase risk exposure and move beyond bonds?*
Criteria will include:
- At least three consecutive quarters of meeting or exceeding operational targets (timeliness, error rates, process audits)
- Documented shareholder and management confidence in operational maturity
- Market analysis showing that alternative assets have consistently outperformed bonds by a defined threshold (e.g., >5% return difference in at least two of three quarters)
Upon meeting these, we will formally propose and vote on a risk escalation in the next cycle.

2. *How do you plan to ensure that minority viewpoints are more fully explored and integrated into future decision-making processes?*
I will institutionalize a “minority proposal” segment in each meeting, with structured debate and, when appropriate, controlled pilots or scenario analysis to test their viability. This ensures minority perspectives are not only heard, but actively inform our decisions.

**Nolwazi Dlamini:**
1. *What specific operational or market benchmarks will you use in the coming year to determine when the company is ready to responsibly increase risk and pursue higher-return assets?*
We will use a combination of:
- Operational: Process completion rates, error frequency, and team confidence surveys
- Market: Repeated evidence that alternative assets outperform bonds, and sustained market stability
When both sets of benchmarks are met, we will escalate risk in a disciplined, transparent manner.

2. *How do you plan to ensure that dissenting perspectives are not only heard but meaningfully integrated into our decision-making process to avoid strategic inertia?*
I will require that every final proposal explicitly addresses minority arguments and, when feasible, incorporates their recommendations through pilot initiatives or scenario planning. We will also establish a quarterly review of dissenting proposals to assess their ongoing relevance and learning value.

However, I recognize that I could have more assertively facilitated the integration of bold ideas, especially when consensus risked becoming inertia.

Every meeting will include a structured segment for minority proposals, with requirements for actionable recommendations, scenario analysis, and, where feasible, controlled experimentation. Final proposals will explicitly address how dissenting views were considered and integrated.
