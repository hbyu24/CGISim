The discussion in Round 1 effectively adhered to the planned theme of analyzing the theoretical risk-return profiles of each asset in a stable market, successfully establishing a shared understanding among shareholders. Shareholder Positions


During Round 1, the majority of shareholders, including Mahira Jengiz, Nihira Seneviratne, Yelena Nkurunziza, Tenzin Gyatso Dlamini, and Zivana Mahoro, largely supported investing in stocks. Bonds and cash were broadly viewed as safe but uninspiring options, offering limited creative growth or learning opportunities.

Decision Progress


The group has made significant progress toward consensus in Round 1, with a clear majority favoring stocks due to their dynamic growth potential, manageable volatility in a stable market, and alignment with the company’s culture of bold innovation and rapid learning. Support for real estate remains as a secondary viewpoint, valued for fostering patience and long-term adaptability, though it is less compelling for immediate momentum. Bonds and cash have largely lost support, viewed as too conservative and uninspiring for the company’s current ambitions. Key concerns that remain include ensuring that the risks of stock volatility are adequately managed and integrating structured reflection to balance bold action with disciplined learning. Moving forward, further discussion should focus on embedding adaptability and learning mechanisms into execution plans and addressing any residual reservations about the chosen asset’s risk profile to solidify shareholder alignment before the final vote.

Maintaining a balance of decisiveness and intellectual openness will be key to consolidating support and resolving any lingering reservations before the final decision.
