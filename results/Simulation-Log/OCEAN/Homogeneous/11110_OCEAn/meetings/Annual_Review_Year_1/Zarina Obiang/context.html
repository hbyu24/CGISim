**2. Responses to Shareholder Questions:**

*From Mireille Akintoye:*

1. **How do you plan to more actively surface and integrate dissenting or minority viewpoints in future decision processes to strengthen risk assessment?**
I will implement a structured “devil’s advocate” round in every major decision process, explicitly inviting shareholders to challenge the prevailing view or present alternative scenarios, regardless of consensus. I’ll also rotate the responsibility for initiating dissent among shareholders to ensure all voices are empowered. This will help us surface hidden risks and test our assumptions more rigorously.

2. **What concrete steps will you implement to ensure our investment strategy captures a higher proportion of available market returns while maintaining disciplined risk management?**
I will introduce real-time performance tracking and quarterly “what-if” reviews, comparing our chosen allocations with actual market outcomes. This will inform mid-year recalibration discussions, allowing us to pivot more confidently when justified by data, while always requiring explicit risk assessment before any major shift.

*From Laleh Abayomi:*

1. **How do you plan to enhance early scenario planning and the surfacing of dissenting views to strengthen our risk preparedness and decision-making in the coming year?**
In addition to the “devil’s advocate” round, I will mandate that each investment proposal be accompanied by at least two plausible alternative scenarios—one optimistic, one pessimistic. We will discuss not just the likely outcome, but also the consequences of being wrong, and predefine triggers for reconsidering our position.

2. **Given the gap between actual and potential returns, what specific steps will you take to increase our investment agility and responsiveness to market signals without compromising our disciplined approach?**
We will formalize mid-year and quarterly “strategy checkpoints” where we review not only performance but also leading indicators from the broader market. Any proposal to change course will require both a data-driven rationale and a clear articulation of risk controls, ensuring agility is always paired with discipline.

*From Zeinab Yılmaz:*

1. **How do you plan to more actively integrate dissenting or minority viewpoints into the decision-making process to strengthen risk assessment and strategic agility?**
I will actively assign the role of “critical reviewer” to a different shareholder each quarter, ensuring that alternative perspectives are not only welcomed but expected. I will also introduce anonymous pre-meeting polling on asset preferences to surface hidden reservations before groupthink can set in.

2. **In light of this year’s missed return opportunities, what specific mechanisms will you implement to ensure more dynamic responsiveness to evolving market data in future quarters?**
We will adopt a “rolling scenario analysis” approach—updating our risk-return projections each quarter and explicitly comparing our current strategy to emerging alternatives. If market data or asset performance diverges from expectations, we will have a standing agenda item to debate potential course corrections, with clear documentation of reasoning and risk tradeoffs.

*From Soraya Jindrak:*

1. **How do you plan to enhance our process for identifying and acting on dissenting viewpoints to ensure we capture a broader range of scenarios and opportunities?**
I will formalize a dissent-tracking log, recording all minority viewpoints and ensuring they are revisited at each decision point. We will also conduct periodic “premortem” exercises—asking, “If this investment fails, what might have caused it?”—to systematically anticipate and plan for downside scenarios.

2. **What specific steps will you implement next year to improve our ability to adjust investment strategy mid-year if market conditions or performance data suggest a better alternative?**
I will schedule mid-year “strategy recalibration” meetings, independent of our regular quarterly reviews, focused specifically on identifying inflection points in market conditions or performance trends. Any proposed adjustment will undergo a rapid scenario stress test before implementation.

Before any move to higher-volatility assets, we will require a formal scenario planning session, mapping out best-case, base-case, and worst-case outcomes, and pre-defining contingency actions for each.
